# FoodIQ — differentiated research hypotheses

These are questions, not assumed findings. The project must report null, contrary or inconclusive results honestly.

## H1 — Food growth quality: orders versus basket value

Does food delivery GOV growth come predominantly from order volume, AOV, or both? The Q4FY26 CEO letter specifically says acceleration was driven more by order/user volumes than AOV. Decompose with source-reported orders/AOV where the table supplies them; validate against the source's GOV series and report residual/rounding.

## H2 — Advertising monetisation as a margin engine

How much of Food's take-rate trajectory and contribution/EBITDA margin expansion is associated with advertising monetisation? Compare reported take-rate and advertising claims, but do not assert causality without a source-disclosed revenue bridge. Extend to Out-of-Home advertising where evidence exists.

## H3 — Quick-commerce basket expansion versus cost of growth

Does AOV / non-grocery mix expansion coincide with NOV growth, order frequency, retention and store productivity? Compare the Q2FY26 non-grocery GOV mix claim (26.2% versus 8.7% a year earlier), AOV, NOV/GOV, orders/store/day and darkstore area.

## H4 — Quick-commerce growth quality and customer pruning

Did culling low-AOV/unprofitable users improve contribution economics? Track MTUs, retention, order growth, frequency, AOV, contribution margin and adjusted EBITDA loss. Distinguish selection effects from genuine improvement in cohort economics.

## H5 — Store maturity and operating leverage

How do active stores, store age/area, orders/store/day, GOV/sqft and store-level contribution-margin disclosures relate to segment contribution margin? Public data are aggregated; any mature-store or top-decile statements must be tagged as management-reported rather than independently calculated.

## H6 — Monthly break-even versus quarterly economics

How did the May 2026 monthly CM break-even milestone coexist with Q1FY27 quarterly CM of -0.2% and Q2FY27 quarterly CM of -1.0%? Examine reinvestment in user habit, category mix, opening-store underutilisation and operating leverage. This is a particularly interview-worthy case study of unit economics and growth reinvestment.

## H7 — Cross-initiative retention and incrementality

Do new propositions such as Toing, No!ce, Switch, Bolt and Eat Right bring truly incremental users or mainly shift existing cohorts? Use disclosed retention/frequency statements as hypotheses and seek cohort-level denominators. Do not claim causal incrementality from “new or dormant” user counts alone.

## H8 — Out-of-Home as an adjacent profitable platform

Can Out-of-Home sustain profitability as it expands active restaurant partners, cities, product experiences and advertising monetisation? Compare quarterly GOV, adjusted revenue, contribution margin and adjusted EBITDA margins; note seasonal fluctuations.

## H9 — Management guidance credibility

How often does Swiggy meet measurable targets, and on what definitions? Separate forecasts from aspirations, milestone achievement from quarter-average performance, and gross from net store additions. Avoid a single simplistic credibility score for incomparable statements.

## H10 — Inventory ownership transition economics

What is the net impact of an IOCC transition on margin, inventory, net working capital, cash conversion and accounting presentation? Scenario-test the stated ~80bps benefit against inventory ownership and working-capital requirements; update only when post-transition actuals exist.

## H11 — Group profitability bridge and capital intensity

How does consolidated adjusted EBITDA evolve alongside B2C GOV, segment EBITDA, Platform Innovations losses, Supply Chain results, capex, working capital, QIP and cash balance? Use the page-16 reconciliation and appendices; distinguish operating cash generation from equity financing and stake monetisation.

## H12 — Competitive lead/lag tests (blocked until Eternal data arrives)

Does Eternal's food AOV / GOV / order momentum precede changes in Swiggy's food economics, or vice versa? Once comparable Eternal letters are loaded, test cross-company lead/lag using aligned quarters, first differences, matched definitions and small-sample warnings. This is not causal inference.

## Additional modules that make the project stand out

1. **Disclosure-to-database provenance graph:** every KPI links back to source document, exact page, table/paragraph, extraction status and reviewer. Provide an audit trail when a report changes a historical observation.
2. **Management claim-to-outcome tracker:** connect management explanations (e.g. “retention improvement,” “delivery density,” “assortment mix”) to subsequent disclosed metrics; classify support as corroborated, contradicted, untestable or partially supported.
3. **Structural-break register:** mark COVID comparability, IPO/QIP financing, segment reclassification, IOCC transition and Lynks disposal. Keep like-for-like and reported series side by side.
4. **Growth quality scorecard:** combine order growth, AOV, repeat/retention, order frequency, discounts/NOV ratio, contribution margin and store productivity. Do not create a composite unless weights and limitations are explicit.
5. **Scenario engine with guardrails:** sensitivity ranges rather than false point precision; explain what a model cannot know from public company data.
6. **Analyst brief with evidence citations:** key findings should link to original PDF/page and disclose whether data is reported, derived, management-stated or assumed.
