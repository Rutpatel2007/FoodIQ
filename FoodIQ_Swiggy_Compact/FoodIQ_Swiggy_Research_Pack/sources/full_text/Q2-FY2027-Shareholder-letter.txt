From the CEO’s desk

Dear Shareholders,

It is my pleasure to present to you our Q2FY27 Shareholders’ letter.

This was a quarter in which our businesses grew faster and our direction became clearer. At our first ever
Capital Markets Day held on 6-Aug-26, we shared our long term aspiration to build towards INR 10,000
Cr of Adjusted EBITDA by FY31, at GOV of over INR 2.5 lakh Cr with ~4% margins. From a headline
perspective, this quarter, B2C GOV grew 24.6% to INR 20,793 Cr, and Consolidated Adjusted Revenue
grew 33.8% to INR 7,909 Cr.

Quick commerce delivered the acceleration in growth as we had indicated. NOV grew 13.4% sequentially
against 2.5% in Q1 to reach INR 6,594 Cr and orders were up 29.3% YoY to 130 million. We added a
healthy base of 1.3 million users during the quarter to reach 14.8 Mn (up 23% YoY) as we focussed on
habit building interventions and hyperlocal actioning while delivering improved frequency and superior
retention metrics. Contribution margin was -1.0% of GOV within the range guided earlier, as we added 69
net stores, taking the network to 1,240 stores across 133 cities, and invested in differentiated assortment,
availability and service quality. The Adjusted EBITDA losses rose by INR 46 Cr YoY to INR 895 Cr. We
expect continued sequential growth in Q3 with operating margin improvement hereon.

As speed and price become table stakes, we believe differentiated assortment will decide the winners.
We presented Switch, which brings together partnerships with ~400 brands, No!ce, our private brand,
now in 1 in 8 baskets with ~15 pp higher retention, and Nectr, our curated fruits and vegetables offering.
For instance, in categories such as high-protein atta, Switch SKUs drove 51% of category growth in four
months. A key update in the quarter was shareholder approval on the cap on foreign shareholding at
49.5% (fully diluted basis) to qualify as an Indian Owned and Controlled entity. The transition will allow
Instamart to own inventory, and is expected to complete over the next few quarters, with a net margin
benefit of about 80 bps.

Food delivery GOV grew 17.7% YoY to INR 10,054 Cr, strongly exiting the quarter with Sep-26 clocking
20%+ YoY growth as we enter the festive quarter. We reported our highest ever Adjusted EBITDA of INR
341 Cr at best ever Adjusted EBITDA margin of 3.4% of GOV (+58 bps YoY). Toing, our standalone
affordability app, is now in 50+ cities, and two in three of its new users are either new to Swiggy or
long-term dormant. The annual guidance of 18-20% for Food delivery (ex -Toing) remains unchanged.



Out-of-Home Consumption grew GOV 34.9% YoY to INR 1,508 Cr, with active restaurants up to 66k (+48%
YoY). The business has now been Adjusted EBITDA-positive for seven consecutive quarters, and we
continue to open Dineout offering in more cities taking the total count to 80+ now. We are also

experimenting with new offerings like dining vouchers, in-restaurant & in-cinema ordering to fuel future
growth.

During the quarter, we advanced our sustainability and social-impact priorities by focusing on enabling
lower-emission deliveries, strengthening delivery-partner welfare and enhancing our ESG governance
and reporting framework. We enabled over 50,000 delivery partners across India to file their Income Tax
Returns (ITR) directly via the delivery partner app for our Food Marketplace and Instamart businesses.
The initiative enabled INR 6.5 Cr of cumulative income tax refund claims for delivery partners.
Additionally, more than one-third of the delivery partners filed returns for the first time, joining the formal
financial ecosystem. To accelerate EV adoption, Instamart co-located battery-swapping at last mile
stations, saving delivery partners up to 60 minutes daily and lowering rental costs by up to 30%.

We also continued to assess emerging regulatory requirements for extended producer responsibility,

Management Perspectives

  1.​ Food delivery YoY growth stood at ~18% in Q2FY27. Does the 18-20% GOV growth guidance
      for the full year still hold in Food Delivery?

     Food delivery GOV grew 17.7% YoY to INR 10,054 Cr in Q2FY27 (+5.9% QoQ). The shape of
     demand in the Food delivery segment continues to evolve. An increasing share of
     affordability-led demand is being served through the 99-Store (on the core Swiggy App) and
     Toing, the dedicated app for budget friendly meals (more on this later) as we continue to expand
     the total addressable market for the segment. In the short term, there were some inter-play of
     demand levers on one platform vs the other which was hard to predict as we built out both the
     offerings.

     Our rapid experimentation over the last 2-3 quarters has given us some clear understanding on
     the use cases of both these offerings and we have been able to accelerate the food delivery
     platform growth (ex Toing) to over 20% in the month of Sep-26 with 20 million+ transacting user
     base. We therefore remain confident of achieving the full year guidance of 18-20% (ex Toing).

  2.​ Food delivery margins (Contribution and Adjusted EBITDA) sequentially improved with higher
      profitability. What led to margin expansion and is it sustainable?

     Food delivery Contribution margin sequentially improved to 7.7% of GOV in Q2FY27 (+9 bps QoQ,
     +34 bps YoY), despite some of the investments towards the affordability thrust. The quarter also
     reported the highest ever absolute Adjusted EBITDA of INR 341 Cr (+INR 49 Cr QoQ) and
     Adjusted EBITDA margins of 3.4% of GOV (+32 bps QoQ, +58 bps YoY). Some of the margin
     expansion levers were a) Advertising-led monetisation that accelerated the take-rate to 26.1%
     during the quarter (+23 bps YoY, highest ever) and b) Delivery cost efficiency through higher order
     density and short-distance orders with increasing penetration of Bolt and 99-Store (collectively
     more than 25% of Food delivery volumes). Operating leverage continues to add to overall margin
     improvement.

     We expect the trajectory to continue towards the stated guidance of 5% steady state Adjusted
     EBITDA margin.

  3.​ What is the scale of Toing and should we expect to see continued investments towards it in
      the near term ? When can we expect more details on economic sustainability of the offering?

     Toing (standalone app for affordable meal options) remains within Platform Innovations, a
     structured home to test new consumer propositions and innovation bets. The growth trajectory,
     value demand unlock and consumer adoption for Toing, while early, have been encouraging as
     we continue to iterate and solve for scalability.




                                                                                               Page 10 of 25
      a)​ Product Market Fit: We continue to see healthy user adoption for the offering
          demonstrating strong product market fit. The success benchmark remains the ability to
          attract new to Swiggy platform users which is demonstrated by 2 out of 3 users on Toing
          being first time Swiggy users or long term dormant on Swiggy. This shows that the
          proposition is adding to the overall category growth which was one of the fundamental
          tenets of launching a separate offering. Even for users who are transacting on both the
          platforms, the combined frequency is ~25% higher than their stand-alone platform
          frequency. ​

      b)​ Category growth expansion: Toing’s success can catapult combined Food delivery
          (Swiggy+Toing) marketplace growth to 25-30% (from the current guided range of 18-20%).
          We are already seeing some of our cities reaching 20-30% of core Food delivery volumes
          in a very short time. The core Swiggy Food delivery profitability equation continues on its
          stated trajectory while Toing can potentially scale on the discretionary incremental
          investments for user acquisition with marginal overheads.​

      c)​ Business market fit: While any new launch necessitates investments in customer
          acquisition channels, user retention levers and under absorbed capacity costs, the
          success of the offering will be determined when unit economics of the repeat users turns
          positive and continues to scale with better monetisation and costs optimisation. We are
          happy to report early green shoots of the business market fit with unit economics turning
          positive for our repeat users in the earliest launched cities.

      d)​ Restaurants: The long tail of value restaurants have benefited the most from Toing,
          observing meaningful uptick in order volumes with improved discovery.

   While these early results are positive, we will need to continue to sharpen the proposition and
   work on monetisation and efficient cost structures to make it sustainable. To reiterate, we are
   applying the same discipline to Toing as to our other new initiatives, continuing to invest only as
   product and business-market fit becomes clearer. Toing runs on our existing tech and execution
   stack with no significant incremental fixed cost, unlike a new entrant building from scratch. As
   mentioned above, the primary investment is on new-user acquisition, which is discretionary and
   can be scaled up or down in an agile manner. Having said that, we do expect the overall
   investment outlay to be range bound in the short term. We shall have more clarity on scalability
   and efficacy of the model in the next 1-2 quarters enabling us to share more details on this.

4.​ What are the changes proposed in GST on delivery fees and impact on the ecosystem?

   In the 57th GST Council meeting held recently, GST on delivery services provided through an
   E-commerce platform by an unregistered person was proposed at 5% instead of the current 18%.

   This change will bring down the overall GST burden on the customers, thereby making the
   platform more affordable for our consumers. The date of the change of GST rate will be known

   days.

5.​ Dineout / Out-of-Home Consumption delivered 35% YoY growth. How should we think about
    the forward looking outlook for the next few quarters?

   Out-of-Home Consumption delivered GOV of INR 1,508 Cr in Q2FY27, up 35% YoY across 66k
   monthly active restaurant partners (+48.1% YoY).

   We continue to launch new frontiers of growth with each new offering opening up a new reason
   to dine out and bringing in new categories of restaurants onto Dineout, making it relevant to
   more occasions. Some of the recent initiatives include:

   Steal Deals lets diners buy a single dish from a restaurant at an accessible price. Selling a dish
   instead of a table makes Dineout far more relevant for quick-service restaurants, cafés, bakeries
   and dessert shops. It also brings in younger, impulse-led diners and extends our reach into
   smaller cities. Digital Menu takes Swiggy from the front door to the table. Diners scan a code,
   then order and pay from their phone, with no waiting for the bill. Cine Dining brings the same
   experience to the cinema, where guests order from their seat and have food delivered. Scenes,
   with its hyperlocal live events and experiences in and around restaurants, bars and cafés,
   completes the offering. Together, they form a complete going-out stack.

   Looking ahead, we expect Contribution and Adjusted EBITDA margin to continue improving
   sequentially on the stated path of 5% Adjusted EBITDA.

6.​ Moving to quick commerce, after a few muted quarters, Q2FY27 saw double digit sequential
    GOV/NOV growth. What factors drove the acceleration and what is the growth outlook from
    hereon?

   Q2FY27 saw acceleration in sequential growth with NOV growing 13.4% QoQ (vs 4% and 2.5% in
   the preceding two quarters) to INR 6,594 Cr with a volume growth of 13.8% QoQ. Three factors
   drove this:

      a)​ Larger and higher quality user base: With the culling of unprofitable users behind us, we
          added 1.3 Mn MTUs in the quarter (our highest addition in five quarters). Month-1 retention
          of the transacting user base averaged over 60% for the quarter which was meaningfully
          higher than the previous quarters. This retention curve along with higher frequency of
          repeat users led to an overall platform frequency increase of 4.2% QoQ.

      b)​ Targeted pricing investments to reactivate dormant base and drive habit building
          behaviours on the platform amongst the repeat users such as the INR 9 Store and
          cohorted pricing interventions. We also made certain tactical investments in speed and
          serviceability during the rainy season that drove higher retentions.



                                                                                            Page 12 of 25
      c)​ Network expansion and differentiated assortment: We added 69 dark stores during the
          quarter (equivalent to the previous 3 quarters’ cumulative count) as higher growth led to
          more stores reaching maximum capacity utilisation. Our differentiated assortment strategy
          led by No!ce and Switch range of products continued to deepen user engagement.

   Competitive intensity in the sector remains high, and we do not expect it to ease in the near-term
   with horizontal e-commerce majors also participating actively through the festive quarter. We
   expect the structural levers of user addition, retention and frequency to deliver another quarter of
   double-digit sequential NOV growth in Q3FY27.

7.​ Contribution margin dipped to -1% of GOV and absolute losses also increased sequentially.
    What were the investment choices made leading to higher operating burn?

   As we had indicated last quarter, having achieved contribution break-even in May’26, we chose
   to calibrate near-term investments to accelerate growth while remaining within our guided range
   of zero to -100 bps on CM. CM moved to -1.0% of GOV in Q2FY27 (-82 bps QoQ, +157 bps YoY).

   Notably, while our take rate declined by 140 bps QoQ, cost efficiencies and operating leverage
   normalised the impact to only 80 bps in contribution margin with the following break-up:​

      a)​ Targeted investments of 100 bps in habit formation and dormant-user reactivation

      b)​ Adverse mix led impact of 40 bps due to change in user mix (larger share of new and
          reactivated cohorts) along with festive season led category mix change, and

      c)​ Operating leverage and other cost efficiencies delivered 60 bps of improvement through
          higher store utilisation with orders per day per store increasing by 9.5% reaching 1,192
          while adding 69 new stores whose under utilisation costs are expensed in contribution
          margin in the first 1-2 quarters leading to contribution margin headwinds in the near term.

8.​ How should we think about balancing growth investment against the path to Adjusted
    EBITDA breakeven guidance?

   The pricing investments highlighted above are discretionary and transient in nature and we
   expect them to start reversing over the next couple of quarters with better retention and maturity
   of cohorts. We believe the Adjusted EBITDA losses to have peaked out in Q2FY27.

   As stated in our previous communication, we expect the contribution margin to remain within the
   zero to -100 bps range in Q3FY27 with sequential improvements thereon. With the double-digit
   sequential NOV growth, we expect the Adjusted EBITDA margin as % of GOV improving by ~100
   bps QoQ in Q3FY27.

   Our path to Adjusted EBITDA break-even remains unchanged. We need ~2.3x today's scale (INR
   60,000 Cr of annualised NOV, from INR 26,400 Cr today) and INR 30 of contribution per order.

   Roughly two-thirds of this will come from higher revenue per order (product mix, own brands and
   advertising) and one-third from lower cost per order (densification, automation and operating
   leverage).

9.​ How is your differentiation strategy, including No!ce and Switch, playing out, and how did it
    contribute to growth this quarter?

   As speed becomes table stakes in quick commerce and aggressive pricing proves unsustainable,
   we believe differentiated assortment will help us build a unique consumer proposition. As we
   shared at our Capital Markets Day, we view assortment as a curation game rather than a volume
   game, and our aim is to enable consumers to buy better, not just offer more.

   The Switch proposition enables this through No!ce, Nectr and partnerships with around 400
   brands to offer superior alternatives across core categories. As of Sep-26, Switch SKUs were part
   of over 20% orders on the platform, with SKUs available in over 60 categories.

       1.​ No!ce: Our clean-food private brand now features in more than 1 in 8 orders across 500+
           SKUs, and customers who buy No!ce show 15pp higher retention. No!ce is often priced at
           or near incumbent SKUs while offering superior quality, which also makes it
           margin-accretive. ​

       2.​ Nectr: Our curated fruits and vegetables proposition is built around one of the most
           habit-forming daily categories. We launched in Bengaluru in Q2FY27, starting with select
           stores and widened coverage through the quarter. As of Sep-26, 1 in 6 of our Bengaluru
           customers bought a Nectr product.​

10.​What is the status of the IOCC transition and the move to inventory ownership in quick
    commerce? What is the likely impact on margin and working capital deployment as a result?

   We would like to thank our shareholders for their overwhelming support for the resolution of
   capping our foreign ownership up to 49.5% (fully diluted basis), helping us transition to an India
   owned and controlled structure. We have since taken necessary regulatory approvals and the
   domestic ownership has risen to over 58%, on the back of growing appetite among domestic
   institutional investors.

   The IOCC transition will start from mid-Q3 FY27, and we expect it to complete over the next 2-3
   quarters. As we have mentioned in our previous communications, this has the potential to add
   ~80 bps to Instamart’s contribution margin when fully rolled-out while giving us greater flexibility
   and control over assortment, pricing, availability and day-to-day operations.

   As we transition to the inventory model, we expect to improve our overall working capital cycle
   by 8-10 days which should offset the additional investment in holding of the inventory on our
   balance sheet. We expect the net working capital days to normalise at ~15 days in this business
   on a steady-state basis. As a result, we do not expect any significant incremental working capital
   deployment in the business. We also do not expect any disruption to the customer experience or


                                                                                              Page 14 of 25
   to our supply relationships during this period and our teams have been preparing the operational
   groundwork in parallel.

11.​ Lynks Logistics was recently sold to Udaan. What was the rationale behind the transaction,
     and how will it affect the reported financials going forward?

   Given our sharpened focus on consumer-facing businesses of Food delivery, Quick commerce,
   and Out-of-Home Consumption, we entered into an agreement to sell our entire shareholding in
   Lynks Logistics Limited, which houses our B2B authorised distribution business, to Trustroot
   Internet Private Limited, the parent entity of Udaan, for a consideration of ~INR 500 Cr in Sep-26.
   Together with a separate primary investment of INR 75 Cr, it will give us a ~3.2% stake in Udaan.

   The Lynks business financials were reported within the “Supply Chain & Distribution” segment.
   Our Q2FY27 reported financial results include the Lynks financials for the full quarter, and will
   cease to be part of our reported results from Q3 FY27 onwards. As a result, we expect the
   segment EBITDA to improve by ~INR 20 Cr from Q3FY27 onwards.
