time to cook is creating demand, not redirecting it. Part of our digital marketing spend on new customer
acquisition could move to such agents, with more targeted CAC than what we currently get from Google
and Meta.

Agents won’t impact profitability/ margins, even if they disintermediate us to some extent

There is a concern that if agents intermediate demand, platform margins will compress.

As mentioned earlier, in categories like food delivery and quick commerce where the supply chain and
infrastructure create the product, the majority of the value will also reside with companies that run these
rails. Eventually, brands and sellers pay us for the sales we generate for them. And if we continue to be the
pipes to power those sales, we will end up being adequately compensated for it, letting us retain our fair
share of margins in the process.​
​
The five ifs​
​
If adoption is narrow, we benefit from AI making our platforms better. ​
If it is broad, our infrastructure is what agents must work through. ​
If agents drive more transparent competition, our execution advantages compound. ​
If they open new categories, our supply chain strengths become the relevant moat. ​
If they generate new demand, our infrastructure fulfills it.

We are conscious that as incumbents, we may be predisposed to see resilience where others see
vulnerability. What gives us a little more peace is also the advantage of observing how this plays out in
markets with higher digital trust before it becomes locally relevant.

We remain aware to not drink our own kool-aid, and we deeply understand that only the paranoid survive.
