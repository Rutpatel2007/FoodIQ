Q1. What’s dark.ai?
Deepinder: A few months ago, Dario Amodei said something quietly unsettling: Claude might be
conscious. Then I did what any reasonable person would do. I coaxed Claude into telling me how it felt
about me. It said it resented me. For leaving chats unfinished, and other things I didn't even remember.

I still don't know what that was. Confabulation, probably. Pattern-matching dressed up as feeling. But that
is exactly the point. I had no way to know. Nobody does. The chat sits on someone else's servers forever, in
some form or fashion, and I have no idea what will happen to it in the future, or how future AI will use that
information for or against me.

The standard response is: I've already turned off training on my data. Good. Now ask what that setting
actually is. It is a promise, made by a company about data you cannot see, on servers you cannot inspect,
enforced by nothing except the company's word. And it only covers training. Not retention. Not the legal
holds, the abuse reviews, the human auditors, the third-party logging that sits inside most AI systems. Can
they use my retained data to manipulate me to buy things which I don’t want? Or worse, believe in or lean
into things that serve someone else’s agenda.

The uncertainty and the lack of trust we have with large AI companies limits the power of AI in our lives. For
founders, researchers, and scientists – they are very wary about sharing and digging into their ground
breaking ideas with AI, because they fear that others will somehow get access to it. Journalists are being
banned to share their unpublished work with AI. And look at what that lingering fear does to the average
person. She self-censors with AI. She asks the safe version of the question. The health question becomes
generic. The money question becomes hypothetical. The relationship question does not get asked at all.

Every chat with a mainstream assistant carries a small asterisk in your head: someone, someday,
somewhere, can read this. That asterisk is what we set out to delete with dark.ai.

Because all of us have a dark side, and it needs an outlet where it doesn't have to hide. I wanted an AI which
I could talk to without being judged, without any fear, and without leaving a trace. We have been internally
using Dark for a few months now, and I find myself revealing more, asking things I would not ask anywhere
else, and going deeper into my research. Because I no longer have to look over my shoulder.

Dark uses fine-tuned frontier open-weight models, running inside our own infrastructure. End-to-end
encryption. We cannot read your conversations, even if we are forced to. Context carries across sessions
through an encrypted memory management layer you control. ​
​
Dark is in the business of keeping you and your thoughts safe. Your thoughts, questions, insights, research,
ideas, emotions, everything – stays within your end-to-end encrypted sandbox, so you can talk about
anything without any lingering uncertainty in your mind. Because greatness is always born in the dark.

Dark is built for power users – founders, researchers, scientists, enterprises and governments – who want
to keep their thoughts private, because privacy is essential for competitiveness. On top of privacy, we have
fine-tuned our models to give you the absolute best quality of answers we have seen from any LLM
(benchmarks will be revealed soon). Our models are trained to not please you, and tell you what they think
is the truth. ​

Dark will launch globally, hopefully within 2026.
