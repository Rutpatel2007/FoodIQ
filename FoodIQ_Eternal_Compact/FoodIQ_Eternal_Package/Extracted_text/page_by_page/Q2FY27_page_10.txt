Albinder: The skepticism usually rests on three assumptions - incomes are lower, basket is smaller, and
density is too thin for the economics to work. Our data so far says the first is true but matters less than
people expect. Data contradicts the other two.

Demand is there in these cities but has been chronically underserved. Organised retail could have served
this demand with greater width of selection and better pricing but it never never fully developed in these
cities. As a result, a lot of what we stock and sell is either not available locally or takes days to arrive
through alternative channels like e-commerce. By making this assortment accessible within minutes, we
create new demand and new behaviors for customers in these cities.

Three data points tell us this is real -

    ●​ Ramp: New stores in these cities reach 1,000 orders per day in similar time as new stores in metros
       (sometimes even faster).
    ●​ Basket: NAOVs are within 85-90% of metros, with baskets also containing categories that we did
       not expect - such as premium beverages, branded diapers, gaming consoles etc.
    ●​ Retention: Customer retention in these cities is comparable to that in the metros.

We also think building the supply to these cities is the harder part and something which will be a moat for
us in the future. For many products we had to build first-mile capability, picking directly from brand
warehouses into our mother warehouses that feed these cities.

We do not expect every small city to look like a metro. Some will stay subscale, and we will be disciplined
about where we expand. But wherever we have built supply, demand has shown up faster and deeper than
we had anticipated. In metros, we usually compete for a market that already exists. In smaller cities, we are
building the market and the supply. That is why we are bullish.

Q9. Store addition seems to have accelerated with 308 net new stores in Q2FY27
taking the total to 2,751 stores. You seem comfortably on track on your guidance
of 3,000 stores by March 2027. What’s the next milestone on this front?
Akshant: We now expect to cross 3,500 stores by March 2027, and our current plans point to about 5,000
stores by March 2028.

Two things give us the confidence to move faster than before -

    1)​ New bets are working. Stores in smaller cities and stores built around newer categories are on the
        right margin and payback trajectory. Together, these will make up the bulk of the expansion.
    2)​ We have a clearer read on competition. Across our more contested markets, new stores are
        clearing our return thresholds even at today's level of competitive intensity.

Our cash balance and profits from both food delivery and quick commerce businesses, also let us fund this
pace of growth comfortably.

Q10. How does this acceleration impact the margins in the near-term and the
NOV growth guidance of 60% CAGR over the next 3 years?
Akshant: We expect Adjusted EBITDA margins to keep improving through this expansion. New stores
weigh on margins (and more so on Contribution) in their early months, but we expect the improvement in
our more mature stores and cities to more than offset that.

That said, we don't manage this business for near-term margins. What matters more is evidence that the
business can reach steady-state margins, and that evidence keeps getting stronger.
