Q6. Where are you in your journey of assortment expansion and how far can you
go? Does broader e-commerce fit into your medium/ long term plans?
Albinder: We are still early. Assortment expansion is one of our highest priorities, because it is the biggest
driver of how often customers come back and how much of their wallet share they bring to us.

We are making good progress. For example, in Delhi NCR, our assortment has grown from about 80,000
SKUs in Q4FY26 to about 100,000 today. We are expanding in two directions:

   ●​ Deeper: more choice within existing categories - more brands, sizes and price points.
   ●​ Wider: categories we don't carry yet.

Wider is harder. Every new category comes with its own supply chain problem. Large appliances need
different storage and handling, fragile and high-value items need different packaging and security, and
some categories have entirely different sourcing and returns. For each one, we have to figure out how
much inventory to hold and where, how it reaches the customer, and whether we can do all that profitably.

On e-commerce, we don't think the line between quick commerce and e-commerce will mean much over
time. For most purchases, if price and choice are similar, customers prefer to get things sooner. Demand is
not the question. The question is whether you can build a supply chain that serves that demand reliably
and profitably. That is the problem we have been solving. We want to bring most of what a household buys
within 10 minutes.

Q7. Can private labels help improve margins further? Why does Blinkit not have a
private label play?
Albinder: A private label has to pass one of two tests - either it fills a gap and brings incremental demand
to the platform, or it improves the economics of demand we already serve, after accounting for everything
it takes to build and market the private label. We have evaluated both and believe it doesn’t pass either of
these tests.

The private label case rests on building something genuinely differentiated - a product customers choose
for its quality, story-telling or design. That means building a brand, not just putting our name on a product -
and involves continuous product development, manufacturing development, quality control, inventory
management across a full portfolio of SKUs, sampling and marketing to drive trial and repeat. We believe
brands will build these capabilities better and more efficiently than us as these would not be within our
core circle of competence.

The exception is where deeper supply-chain control itself creates value. Fruits and vegetables are the
clearest case - and Hyperpure already gives Blinkit that benefit through sourcing and quality control,
without a separate consumer-facing brand.

Blinkit is the distribution and discovery platform for thousands of brands, many of them emerging. The
shelf space and visibility we would spend marketing an own label can instead help other brands grow on
our fair and neutral platform. This also allows us to earn advertising revenue as the brands grow. We don’t
want to become both the platform and a competitor to the brands on the platform. We would rather avoid
that conflict unless the growth or margin case for owning the label is clearly compelling.
